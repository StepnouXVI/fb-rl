\section{Low-Level Execution: Quadruped Dynamics vs Kinematic Straight-Line Motion}

A frequent intuitive question arises in hierarchical navigation: \textit{Why cannot the agent simply move in a straight Euclidean line towards the next waypoint using coordinate tracking, rather than relying on a complex latent intention policy $\pi^\ell(a \mid s_t, z)$?}

In this section, we provide a rigorous physical and mathematical analysis of this question.

\subsection{Physical Underactuation and Torque Control in MuJoCo}
The \texttt{antmaze} environment simulates an 8-DoF continuous torque-controlled quadruped robot modeled by the non-linear rigid body equations of motion:
\begin{equation}
    M(q)\ddot{q} + C(q, \dot{q})\dot{q} + G(q) = B \tau + J_c(q)^\top \lambda_c
\end{equation}
where $q \in \mathbb{R}^{15}$ (3D base position, quaternion orientation, and 8 joint angles), $\tau \in [-1, 1]^8$ are direct actuator torques, and $\lambda_c$ represents unilateral contact reaction forces with the ground.

Crucially, the $(x, y)$ planar coordinates of the torso are \textbf{unactuated}: there are no thrusters or wheels acting directly on the center of mass. To generate a linear displacement $\Delta (x, y)$, the robot must:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
    \item Execute a dynamic rhythmic gait cycle (trot / crawl) alternating swing and stance leg phases;
    \item Maintain static and dynamic stability (Zero Moment Point within the support polygon) to prevent tumbling;
    \item Coordinate contact normal and frictional forces with ground reaction constraints.
\end{enumerate}

A naive kinematic PD controller $\tau = K_p (w - s_{xy}) - K_d v_{xy}$ mapped directly to joint torques causes immediate leg flailing, loss of ground traction, and catastrophic body inversion (the ant flips onto its back within 5--10 steps, terminating in failure).

\subsection{The Role of $\pi^\ell(a \mid s, z)$ as a Latent Motor Controller}
The frozen low-level policy $\pi^\ell(a \mid s_t, z)$ acts as an expert motor controller trained via goal-conditioned RL to translate direction intentions $z \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ into stable, torque-efficient walking gaits. 

When the high-level planner provides an intention $z$ aligned with the forward direction of the corridor:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
    \item $\pi^\ell$ adjusts leg swing phases to tilt the torso in the direction of the vector;
    \item It maintains forward momentum $v \approx 1.2\text{--}1.8\text{ m/s}$;
    \item In corners, $z_{\text{cmd}}$ rotates the torso azimuth before advancing, preventing corner clipping.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Alternative Optimization in Latent FB Space}
Rather than bypassing $\pi^\ell$, one can formulate online trajectory optimization directly in the latent FB space via projected gradient ascent:
\begin{align}
    z^* &= \arg\max_{z \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}} \mathcal{J}(z) \quad \text{where } \mathcal{J}(z) = F(s_t, z)^\top B(w_1) \\
    z_{k+1} &= \text{Normalize}\left(z_k + \eta \nabla_z \left[ F(s_t, z_k)^\top B(w_1) \right] \right)
\end{align}

\begin{keyinsight}[Key Physical Takeaways]
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Naive Joint PD Controller Fails Completely (0\% Success, 0.05 m/s)}: Without coordinated gait cycles, static friction locks the quadruped in place.
    \item \textbf{FB Gradient Ascent Maximizes Straight Corridor Progress (60\% vs 20\%)}: Direct optimization on $F(s, z)^\top B(w_1)$ outperforms the regression-trained \texttt{high\_actor} on straightaways.
    \item \textbf{Local Single-Step Optimization Stalls on Corners}: Optimizing solely for the immediate point $w_{k+1}$ cuts corners into inner walls. This confirms that \textbf{Sequence-Aware contextual conditioning} is strictly required to navigate multi-turn mazes.
\end{enumerate}
\end{keyinsight}
